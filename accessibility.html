Accessibility statement

Last reviewed September 1, 2026. We build this site to the Web Content Accessibility Guidelines 2.1 at Level AA. This page says what is done, what we know is still imperfect, and how to reach a person if something here blocks you.

Conformance status

Halden Field aims to conform to WCAG 2.1 Level AA. We assess ourselves as substantially conformant, based on internal testing rather than a third-party audit. We draw that distinction plainly, because self-assessment is weaker evidence than an external audit, and we would rather tell you where the claim comes from than imply a certification we do not hold.

What is implemented

  • Semantic structure. Every page uses landmark elements (header, nav, main, footer) and a single logical heading order, so screen reader users can navigate by heading and region.
  • Skip to content. A skip link is the first focusable element on every page and becomes visible when focused.
  • Keyboard operability. Every interactive control, including the mobile menu, filters, size pickers, accordions, the bag drawer and the cookie preferences dialog, can be reached and operated with a keyboard. Open panels trap focus and close on Escape, returning focus to the control that opened them.
  • Visible focus. A three-pixel high-contrast outline is applied to every focused element. We have not removed focus styling anywhere.
  • Color contrast. Body text and interface text meet or exceed 4.5:1 against their background. Large display text meets or exceeds 3:1. Our accent color is used as a background behind dark text rather than as light text on a light surface, because that combination fails.
  • Color is never the only signal. Selected filters and sizes carry a pressed state that screen readers announce, not just a color change.
  • Images. Every content image has descriptive alternative text. Decorative elements are implemented in CSS and hidden from assistive technology.
  • Forms. Every field has a visible label, not a placeholder acting as one. Errors appear next to the field they belong to, are announced to assistive technology, and describe how to fix the problem.
  • Motion. Every animation is disabled when your system requests reduced motion. Nothing on this site flashes, and no content moves automatically for more than five seconds.
  • Zoom and reflow. Pages reflow to a single column at 320 pixels wide and support 200% text zoom without loss of content or function. Pinch zoom is not disabled.
  • Target size. Interactive controls are at least 44 by 44 pixels with spacing between them.
  • Tables. Size and shipping tables use proper header cells with scope attributes and a caption.
  • Language. The document language is declared on every page.

Known gaps

We would rather write these down than claim the site is finished.

  • No third-party audit. Our conformance claim is based on internal testing with keyboard navigation, VoiceOver, NVDA and automated tooling. An external audit is planned but has not been completed.
  • Product photography alternative text is descriptive rather than exhaustive. If you need a fuller description of how a garment looks or is constructed, email us and a person will write one for you.
  • Long size and shipping tables require horizontal scrolling on small screens. The tables are marked up correctly and are readable by screen readers, but the scrolling is not ideal.
  • The checkout step is provided by our payment processor. Its accessibility is outside our direct control, though we have chosen a provider that publishes a conformance report.
  • Documents sent by email, such as line sheets for wholesale partners, are not yet tagged PDFs. Ask and we will send the content as accessible HTML or plain text.

How we test

  • Keyboard-only navigation of every template before release.
  • Screen reader passes with VoiceOver on macOS and iOS, and NVDA on Windows.
  • Automated checks on every page for contrast, landmarks, labels and alt text.
  • Text zoom to 200% and viewport testing down to 320 pixels wide.
  • Verification that everything still works with reduced motion enabled and with JavaScript disabled, where the feature allows it.

If something blocks you

Tell us and we will fix it. We aim to acknowledge accessibility reports within one business day, and to resolve the problem or give you a working route around it within ten business days. If it is going to take longer than that, we will say so rather than leave you waiting.

Standards referenced

  • Web Content Accessibility Guidelines (WCAG) 2.1, Level AA.
  • Section 508 of the Rehabilitation Act, which incorporates WCAG 2.0 Level AA by reference.
  • The Americans with Disabilities Act, as it is applied to commercial websites.
