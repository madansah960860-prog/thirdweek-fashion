Four numbers, plainly

Reading a knit spec

Every knit on this page carries the same four figures. They are worth ten minutes of your attention, because between them they tell you how warm a piece will be, how it will sit, and what it will look like after three winters of use.

Weight, in gsm

  • What it is: grams of cloth in a square meter. The single best guide to warmth
  • Under 200: next to the skin, or a summer layer. The Featherweight Tee is 150
  • 200 to 320: the working middle. Merino Ridge is 250, Ribline is 310
  • Over 350: outer layers you stop moving in. Loop Terry is 380, the hoodie 420

Micron, for wool only

  • What it is: the thickness of a single wool fiber. Lower is finer and softer
  • Under 18: comfortable directly against the neck. The merino tee is 17.5
  • 18 to 19: still soft, harder wearing. Merino Ridge is 18.5
  • Over 21: the itch most people remember from school jumpers. We do not use it

Gauge and structure

  • What it is: how many stitches sit in an inch, and how they are arranged
  • Fine gauge: smooth, layers flat under a shell, shows its shape
  • Structured: waffle and grid lift cloth off skin so damp can move
  • Rib: 2 x 2 rib gives stretch across the chest and pulls back at the waist

Blend, and what it costs you

  • Nylon in wool: added for abrasion life, at the price of a little softness
  • Elastane in trims: keeps a cuff and hem in shape, dies in a hot dryer
  • Cotton in terry: heavy and slow to dry, and pleasant for exactly that reason
  • Lyocell in a blend: drape and cool handling, a little more shrink on first wash

Patina is not damage

Three things happen to a good knit and none of them are faults. Garment-dyed cotton fades first at the cuffs and elbows, which is where your hands go. Wool blends pill lightly under the arms in the first season, and a cheap fabric comb takes it off in a minute. Heavy terry softens and loses a fraction of its length, which is why we cut the pattern long. A knit that still looks new after two years has probably been sitting in a drawer.

When to reach for wool over fleece

Wool, briefly

Merino does not need a wash after every outing. Air it overnight and the smell goes, because the fiber takes odor in and lets it out again rather than holding bacteria the way a synthetic does. When it does need washing, cold water and a flat dry keep the fibers from felting, and a mesh bag keeps them from catching. Our layering notes cover when to reach for wool over fleece.
