Reading the ratings

How we rate weather

Three words do most of the work on these pages: showerproof, stormproof and wind resistant. They are not grades we invented to sell the expensive one. Each is tied to a number you can check above, and each has a point where it stops.

Showerproof

  • The number: 5,000mm hydrostatic head, or a repellent finish with no rating at all
  • Holds: Drizzle, wet snow, spray off a road, half an hour of light rain
  • Stops when: The rain settles in, or a strap presses wet fabric against your shoulder
  • Here: Longshadow Anorak at 5,000mm, Slipstream Wind Vest with no rating

Stormproof

  • The number: 20,000mm hydrostatic head with every seam taped
  • Holds: Hours of steady rain, sideways wind, a whole day outside in it
  • Stops when: Water stops beading on the face fabric, which a warm tumble usually puts right
  • Here: Kestrel Shell Jacket and Rainshadow 2.5L Jacket

Wind resistant

  • The number: quoted in miles per hour, not millimeters, because it is a different test
  • Holds: Open ground and long descents. The reflective shell is rated to 25 mph
  • Stops when: You need rain kept out too. Wind resistance is not waterproofing
  • Here: the reflective shell, and the windproof front panel of the Slipstream vest

The number nobody prints on the hanger

Waterproof ratings tell you what stays out. Breathability tells you what gets away, counted in grams of moisture through a square meter in 24 hours. The Kestrel sits at 15,000g and the Rainshadow at 20,000g, and in plain terms that is the difference between opening the vents early and being able to leave them shut. A shell that has gone damp on the inside feels exactly like one that has leaked, so when the air is warm and wet, take the lighter layer and let the wind do the work.

Not sure which one

Kestrel is the lightest and packs down smallest. Rainshadow is the one for a full day of rain. Longshadow and Slipstream deal with wind and a passing shower, and neither is waterproof. If you are between two of them, tell us the weather you actually train in and we will say which one we would take out the door.
