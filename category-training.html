Repetition and repair

A week in the line

Six garments have to cover seven days, and none of them gets a rest. This is how a normal week runs through them, where the wear shows up first, and what to do about it before you reach for a replacement.

Monday to Wednesday: the easy days

  • Worn: the 5 in short and the Session Tee, then the same again
  • Why it matters: these two carry more than half the mileage in the line
  • Wash: cold, inside out, both dry by the evening
  • If you own one of each: the laundry sets the schedule, not you

Thursday: the hard one

  • Worn: the singlet at 105 gsm when it is warm, the tee when it is not
  • Why it matters: bonded shoulders mean nothing rubs under a pinned number
  • Wash: gentle and in a mesh bag, then line dry only
  • Note: light colors read semi-sheer when the cloth is soaked

Saturday: the long day

  • Worn: half tights above 45 degrees, full tights below it
  • Why it matters: the wind panel sits on the front of the thigh, where cold lands
  • Wash: cold and line dried. Heat kills a laminate and coats elastane
  • Never: fabric softener, on anything with stretch in it

Sunday: standing still

  • Worn: the sweatpant, 400 gsm, over whatever you finished in
  • Why it matters: staying warm afterwards is the quiet half of the week
  • Wash: tumble low and take it out slightly damp
  • Expect: up to 3% shrink in length once, already allowed for in the pattern

What goes first, and what to do about it

Three things fail before anything else, and none of them means the garment is finished. A drawcord frays at the tip, and a knot or a dab of clear glue holds it for years. Elastane in a waistband slackens if it has been dried hot, so drop the heat before you replace the short. A liner is the first thing to go thin, and it is also the reason we run 50 wash cycles on every training piece before it reaches this page, with 12 athletes over an 8 week block deciding whether it holds. If something does give out early, tell us what happened and where, because that is the information that changes the next pattern.

Tell the field desk

Why two of everything

Most people who train four or more times a week end up with two pairs of shorts and two tees in rotation. That is an honest recommendation rather than an upsell: with one of anything, your washing machine sets the calendar instead of you, and a garment that never gets a day to dry out properly wears through sooner.
