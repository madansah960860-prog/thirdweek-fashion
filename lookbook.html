Volume 06

Longshadow

March light comes in low here, and for about forty minutes everything on the river road has a shadow twice its own length. We shot for four mornings inside that window and took the weather as it arrived.

See Volume 06 Read the field notes

The brief fit on one card

Photograph the clothes in use, in the weather they were drawn for, on people who are out four or more mornings a week anyway. No stylists, no borrowed shoes, no water sprayed on a shoulder to look like effort. Everyone wore their own things alongside ours and ran their own routes, which is why the frames are not tidy.

We started at 6:10 a.m. on four consecutive mornings to hold the light steady. Two came up wet. One was cold and clear enough to see the far ridge. The fourth arrived under fog thick enough that the set is mostly silhouette, and we kept all of it, because an Asheville March is not one kind of morning.

Longshadow is named for what that hour does. Low sun, long throw, everything on the road stretched out ahead of itself. It lasts about forty minutes and then the town wakes up.

Where and when

  • Ground: West Asheville, the River Arts District, and the greenway along the French Broad
  • Mornings: March 3 to March 6, 2026, starting at 6:10 a.m. each day
  • Weather: Two wet, one clear at 28 degrees, one under river fog
  • On the road: Members of three Asheville run clubs, paid a day rate and fed afterwards
  • Worn here: Longshadow Anorak, Session Tee, Switchback Tights, Kestrel Shell, Ridgeline Field Cap

Who made these pictures

Production

  • Photography: shot on 35mm colour negative and a digital body, daylight only, no supplementary lighting on any frame
  • Direction: Halden Field, in house, with our product development lead on the road each morning
  • Crew: five people, two cars and a flask, which is the whole unit
  • Post: exposure, contrast and colour balance. Nothing added, nothing taken out

On the road

  • Runners: eleven members of three Asheville clubs, paid a day rate and named in the printed volume
  • Styling: none. Everyone chose their own socks, shoes and hats
  • Routes: the ones they already run, at the hour they already run them
  • Food: afterwards, every morning, at a place on Haywood Road

Rules we shot under

  • No retouching of anybody's body, at any stage
  • No clips, tape or pins on a garment. If a piece needs a clip to sit right, the pattern is wrong
  • No weather made in post. The wet frames were shot in rain and the fog is fog
  • No quote published unless the person said it and read it back

How we work, in more detail

Why the pictures look like this

A lookbook is the easiest place in a company to start lying, so we set the limits before the first morning rather than in the edit.

  • Nobody is reshaped. We correct exposure and colour balance and stop there. The bodies in these frames are the bodies that were on the road
  • Nothing is pinned. A garment that needs a clip at the back to photograph well is a pattern we have not finished
  • Nobody is borrowed. Everyone here runs regularly, was asked in advance, and was paid
  • The weather is the weather. Four mornings, taken in the order they came, including the one we could barely see through
  • No invented praise. If a quote appears under a photograph, the person said it and approved the wording

The last one matters more than it sounds. Writing a warm sentence and putting a runner's name under it takes about a minute and costs nothing. We would rather show the clothes working through a wet week and let you make up your own mind.
