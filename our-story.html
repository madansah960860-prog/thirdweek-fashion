Where this started

Halden Field began at a mending table.

In the winter of 2017 our founders took over a corner of a run shop in West Asheville and offered free repairs on Thursday evenings. Zips, cuffs, seams, waistbands, pocket bags. Whatever came through the door. It was meant to last one season. It ran for two winters, and by the end there were four notebooks on the shelf above the table.

The notebooks turned out to be the point. Every repair got written down: the garment, the season it was bought, the miles the owner reckoned it had done, and where it gave out. Two winters in, the pattern was not subtle. Fabric almost never failed. What failed was a zip slider, a bar tack at the pocket bag, an elastic that had lost its recovery, a cuff gone slack. Small parts, in the same few places, on garments that were otherwise sound and often barely faded.

That is an argument for building differently, and for building less. Halden Field was incorporated in Asheville in 2019 around one piece, the crew that became the Emberline Grid Crew, and one rule that still holds: if a part is going to wear out first, it has to be a part we can replace.

We would rather make thirty things that come back for a repair than a hundred that come back for a refund.

How a garment gets made here

Every piece moves through the same five stages, at a pace we have stopped apologising for. From the first note to a live product page usually runs eleven to sixteen months. Two volumes a year is what that pace allows, and we are not looking for a way around it.

  1. The repair note

    Every piece starts from something that wore out. We write down what gave way, on what garment, after how long, and what the replacement part would cost. That note is the brief. There is no mood board stage.

  2. Fabric trials

    Candidate fabrics are tested for abrasion, pilling, colorfastness and dimensional stability across fifty wash cycles. Anything that moves more than 3% in either direction is set aside, however good it feels on the roll.

  3. Pattern and first sample

    Patterns are graded across the full XS to XXL range rather than scaled up and down from one midpoint. Grading from a single size is why so much apparel fits well in the middle and poorly at both ends.

  4. A season of wear

    Twelve wear testers take the sample out for eight weeks across mixed climates and log every outing. A failure stops the run rather than starting a revision. Four pieces have been shelved this way since 2019.

  5. Small runs, one price

    We cut in small runs with two partner factories, publish the composition and the weight, and set the price on landed cost plus a fixed margin. No anchor price, no markdown calendar, no quiet increase ahead of a sale.

The business, in plain terms

We sell direct, in US dollars, from this site. That is nearly all of it. The rest is a short list of stockists and some gift cards.

Direct to you

  • Every retail order is placed here and packed by us in Asheville
  • A price is set once and then left alone. There is no seasonal markdown calendar
  • With no wholesale margin to protect, the $268 Kestrel Shell is priced on what it costs to land, not on a department store markup
  • No flash sales, and no raising a price so that it can be discounted later

A seasonless core, two volumes a year

  • Twenty-two of our thirty styles are core, and they stay in stock through the year
  • Core styles are restocked rather than replaced, usually every six to eight weeks
  • Two volumes a year. Volume 06 released March 6, 2026. Volume 07 opens October 2, 2026 at 9:00 a.m. Eastern
  • A volume is cut in a fixed quantity and is not restocked once it sells through

Slow Line, cut after you order

  • Cut and sewn in Asheville once the order is in, on the same benches as our samples
  • Lead time is currently 18 to 24 business days, published on every Slow Line product page
  • Payment is taken at order, because the fabric is cut for your size and no one else
  • Exchangeable for another size within 30 days. Not eligible for a refund

Stockists

  • A short list of independent run and outdoor shops carries the core line
  • Opening minimum, margin and lead times are published rather than negotiated shop by shop
  • We do not list on marketplaces or third-party resale platforms
  • Shops can apply through the stockist page

What shipping and returns cost us

  • Free US standard shipping over $125, flat $8 below it. The threshold sits at our real average parcel cost, not at a number chosen to lift baskets
  • 30 days to return or exchange, unworn with tags
  • Exchanges: we cover the freight both ways, once per order
  • Refund returns: a $7 label fee comes off the refund. Someone has to pay the freight, and we would rather it was the parcel that caused it than every price on the site

Gift cards and referrals

  • Digital gift cards from $25 to $500, with no expiry date and no dormancy fee
  • Referrals: your friend gets $20 off a first order, you get $20 once it ships
  • No commission paid to anyone on an individual sale
  • The detail sits on the gift cards page

The company, honestly sized

Halden Field is fourteen people. Four in design and development, three in operations and fulfillment, two in customer support, two at the Slow Line benches, and three across finance, marketing and everything nobody else picked up. We are not a large company dressed as a small one, and we are not a small company posing as a large one.

Everything ships from our own floor on Riverside Drive, a few minutes from the water. When you write to support, the reply comes from someone in that room who can stand up, walk twenty feet, and look at the piece you are asking about.

Four limits we keep

What we will not do

We will not add a third volume

Two a year is the ceiling, and the eight-week wear test is what sets it. A third volume would mean shortening that test or skipping it, and the test is the only reason we trust what goes on the site. The calendar can wait for the clothes.

We will not sell a piece we cannot repair

If a zip, a cuff or a waistband cannot be replaced on our own bench, the design goes back a stage. That rules out constructions we genuinely like, including a few bonded seams that photograph beautifully and cannot be opened again once they are closed.

We will not let the line outrun the testing

Thirty styles is roughly what twelve testers can cover properly in a year. Growing the line means growing the test group first, in that order. We have turned down ideas we liked on exactly these grounds, and we expect to again.

We will not fly production in

Bulk goods move by sea, even when a date slips because of it. Volume 06 landed two weeks late for this reason and we let it. We would rather move a launch than move a container through the air to protect one.

Rules we wrote down early

  • No manufactured urgency. No countdown timers, no banner counting down the last two. A piece is either available or it has sold through
  • No invented reviews. We do not publish praise we wrote ourselves, and we do not show a rating we cannot trace back to a real order
  • No number we cannot show you. When we print 20,000mm, that is a lab result from the mill, and the report goes to anyone who asks for it
  • No language about your body. Our copy describes garments. It does not describe the people wearing them
  • No quiet traps. Unsubscribing takes one click, the cookie banner has a real reject button, and nothing slides into your bag on its own

None of this is positioning. They are constraints that cost us money in a way we can measure, and we carry the cost because the version of this company without them is not one we would want to work at.
