Emberline Grid Crew

$128

Grid fleece works because of what is left out. The knit holds open channels against the skin, so warm air settles in the pockets while moisture travels along the grid lines and leaves. An ember keeps its heat long after the flame has gone, and that is the whole job of this layer: warmth that stays put while you work, and a piece that is dry again by the time you are.

This is the piece most people here own two of. It goes under the Kestrel Shell through the cold months and stands on its own from roughly 45 degrees Fahrenheit upward. It is seasonless, so it carries through volumes rather than arriving and leaving with one.

Fabric

One knit, from one mill, run to the same specification every year so a crew bought in 2019 still matches the one bought last week.

  • Body: 92% recycled polyester, 8% elastane grid fleece, 190 gsm
  • Odor control: A mineral treatment bonded into the yarn rather than a surface coating, so it is still there after a hundred washes
  • Cuffs and collar: Same fabric, bound rather than ribbed, so they do not stretch out
  • Weight: 9.2 oz (261 g) in men's medium

Fit

Close enough to layer under a shell, loose enough that the grid channels stay open and keep doing their work.

  • Cut: Slim. It is a mid layer, so it sits close enough to layer under a shell
  • Seams: Flatlock throughout, offset from the shoulder so a pack strap does not sit on them
  • Length: Standard body length, straight hem
  • On the model: 5 ft 10 in tall, 38 in chest, wearing size M with nothing over it

Care

Grid fleece asks for very little. The one thing that ruins it is softener, so it is worth saying twice.

  • Wash: Machine wash cold, inside out, with like fabrics
  • Dry: Tumble low or line dry. Fleece dries fast, high heat is unnecessary
  • Avoid: Fabric softener. It fills the grid channels and the fabric stops moving moisture
  • Pilling: Minimal on this knit. If any appears at the cuff, a fabric comb takes it off in a minute

Sizing

Unisex sizing, measured in inches. How you plan to wear it matters more here than your usual size.

  • Range: XS to XXL with US numeric equivalents
  • Between sizes: Take the larger size if it will be your outer layer, the smaller if it lives under the shell
  • Chest for size M: Fits 37 to 40 in body chest

From the field desk

Eight weeks, then the washing machine

A mid layer is easy to get right on the first wear and hard to get right on the fiftieth. Warmth, loft and odor control all live or die in the wash, so the test block for this crew spends as long in a laundry room as it does outdoors.

  1. Eight weeks on twelve people

    Twelve runners wear the crew through a full winter block, in whatever they would have worn anyway. They are asked to report what annoys them, not what they like.

  2. Fifty wash cycles, minimum

    Every sample goes through at least fifty cycles before we sign anything off. We measure shrinkage, loft in the grid channels, and how much the odor treatment has faded. Anything that moves more than 3% in either direction goes back to the mill.

  3. The seams get worn under a pack

    The flatlock seams were moved off the shoulder line during this block, after two testers came back with a rub mark from a hydration strap on a long day.

Why it takes a season to release a crew

We publish two volumes a year, and a seasonless piece like this one only changes when testing says it should. If a sample fails at week six, it waits for the next block rather than going out on the promise of a fix.

The rest of the detail

Grid fleece is the better answer when you are working hard and making a lot of moisture, because it moves and dries faster than wool. Merino is the better answer for multi-day wear, for slow cold days when you are not generating much heat, and for trips where staying fresh over several days matters more than drying quickly. We sell both, and we would rather you bought the right one once.

Our layering article walks through the choice in full, including where each fabric lets you down.

  • Flatlock seams offset from the shoulder line
  • Bound neck and cuffs, no ribbing to lose shape
  • Reflective heat transfer at the centre back, roughly 2 in wide, for the dark end of a winter afternoon
  • No zip pockets. Pocket bags sit as a hard lump under a shell, and this piece is usually under one
