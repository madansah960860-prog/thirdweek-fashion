Ridgeline Field Cap

$42

A ridgeline is the simplest line to follow when the light is going, and this is the simplest cap we know how to make. Five panels, a brim soft enough to fold into a jacket pocket and stubborn enough to come back out straight, and laser-cut vents through both side panels. The band is a brushed knit that holds moisture instead of sending it into your eyes.

The rear panel is elastic rather than a plastic snap. There is no hard edge under a hood, nothing to catch on the arms of sunglasses, and nothing that cracks in the third winter.

Fabric

Four parts, all of them chosen so the cap can be packed flat and worn the same day.

  • Crown and brim: 100% recycled polyester ripstop, 40 denier
  • Vents: Laser-cut, no mesh insert to trap grit
  • Brow band: Brushed polyester knit with a silicone gripper strip
  • Brim core: Flexible EVA, folds and recovers
  • Weight: 1.9 oz (54 g)

Fit

Low and quiet on the head. It is meant to disappear about a mile in.

  • Size: One size, elastic rear panel fits 21 to 24 in head circumference
  • Brim: 2.75 in, angled slightly down to hold off low morning sun
  • Profile: Low crown, sits close rather than tall
  • On the model: 22.5 in head circumference, worn with no adjustment

Care

A cap picks up more salt than anything else you own, and salt is what ages it. Rinsing is most of the care.

  • Wash: Cold hand wash or cold gentle cycle in a mesh bag
  • Dry: Air dry on a shaped surface, never tumble
  • Avoid: Bleach, which yellows the reflective trim
  • Salt: Rinse in cool water after hot days, before salt rings set into the brim

Sizing

One size, held by elastic rather than a row of snap holes. Measure once and you will know.

  • Range: One size only, no S or L split
  • How to measure: Tape around the head just above the ears and eyebrows
  • Above 24 in: Write to us before you order and we will tell you honestly whether it will sit right
  • Full chart: Size guide, accessories section

The long view

How a cap ages, and what we do about it

A cap is the cheapest thing we make and usually the piece people keep longest. It takes the most sun, the most salt and the most washing, so we tested it the way it gets used: eight weeks on twelve runners, then fifty wash cycles to see what the brim and the band did afterwards. Everything below is what we learned, and what we are willing to do about it later.

What wears first

  • The band, which absorbs salt and eventually stiffens
  • The reflective trim, which dulls before it lifts
  • The crown fabric, which fades on the side that meets the sun
  • The brim core outlasts all three, which is why it is the part we did not compromise

What we repair

  • Band replacement, at cost plus shipping, for as long as we hold the part
  • Restitching a lifted panel seam or a loosened vent edge
  • Elastic replacement in the rear panel
  • Send it to the Asheville address in the footer and we will write back with a price first

When it is finished

  • A faded cap is not a failed cap. Most of ours look better at year three
  • When it genuinely will not hold a shape, post it back to us
  • We separate the EVA brim core from the fabric and route each into textile recycling
  • Nothing sent back to us goes to landfill

We would rather sell you one cap twice as slowly

Repair is not a favour we do at the end. It is why the rear panel is elastic instead of a snap strap, why the vents are cut rather than glued in, and why the band is stitched so it can be taken out without opening the crown. A piece built to be fixed is a piece designed differently from the start.

How we handle materials and end of life

The rest of the detail

A stiff brim looks better on a shelf. It also cannot be folded into a jacket pocket, and it cracks the first time you sit on it. Ours uses a flexible EVA core that takes a fold and comes back. The trade-off is real and we would rather state it: in a strong headwind this brim flexes a little more than a rigid one.
