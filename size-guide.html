Size guide

Every number on this page describes a body rather than a garment, and every one of them is in inches. Take your own measurements, find the row you sit in, then read how the piece you are looking at is cut to sit. If you land on the line between two rows, the advice further down is written for the garment rather than offered as a shrug.

Taking your own measurements

  1. Chest

    Run the tape around the fullest part of the chest and under the arms, keeping it level across the back. Arms down at your sides. Breathe out normally rather than holding it in, or the jacket will be tight on every ordinary day afterwards.

  2. Natural waist

    The narrowest part of the torso, which for most people sits about an inch above the navel. Let the tape rest where it wants to sit. Pulling it in by half an inch only moves the problem to the garment.

  3. Hip

    Around the fullest part of the seat with your feet together and the tape level to the floor. A mirror helps more than a second pair of hands here.

  4. Inseam

    Easiest measured off clothing rather than off a leg. Take a pair of pants that already fit you, lay them flat, and measure from the crotch seam down the inside leg to the hem.

  5. Head

    Around the widest part of the head, roughly one finger width above the eyebrows and passing over the top of the ears. That is where a cap band actually sits.

When you land between two rows

We grade every pattern across the whole range rather than scaling one middle size up and down, so the answer is not simply take the bigger one. What matters is what the piece is for and what is going underneath it. Fit here is meant to hold up over years and seasons, not to look right for one photograph, and a layer you can move in on a cold morning will outlast one that only fits standing still.

Go smaller when

  • The piece is meant to sit close to the body and nothing is going under it
  • The garment is already described as relaxed, or cut with a dropped shoulder
  • You are between rows on the chest but at the lower end of the waist range
  • The cloth carries 8% or more elastane, which stretches and comes back

Go larger when

  • A mid layer is going under the shell on cold mornings
  • You sit at the top of the chest range and want your arms free overhead
  • The cloth is a woven with no stretch in it, such as the Longshadow Anorak
  • You would rather knitwear skimmed the body than held it

Still on the fence

Send your chest, waist and height, and name the piece. Sizing mail is answered within one business day by someone who has handled the garment. If we think it will not work on you, we will say so, including on the days when the honest answer is that another maker cuts closer to your proportions than we do.
